## 1. Group decision-making process for teams

Type: article. Series: guides. Query: `group decision making process`.
Angle: collect input, surface dissent, and document the final call.

Proposed factual anchors:

1. Atlassian's DACI framework distinguishes Driver, Approver, Contributors,
   and Informed roles; one Approver makes the decision.
2. Its decision document includes relevant evidence, evaluation factors,
   and the options considered.
3. Its follow-up procedure records the final decision and the reasons behind it.

Source: [Atlassian Team Playbook: DACI](https://www.atlassian.com/team-playbook/plays/daci),
role definitions, action-plan instructions, and follow-up. DACI means Driver,
Approver, Contributors, and Informed. These facts describe that specific framework,
not a requirement that every group use one decision-maker. The article's practical
workflow will be clearly presented as guidance, not a validated productivity study.

## 2. Decision matrix template for team choices

Type: article. Series: templates. Query: `decision matrix template`.
Angle: provide a reusable template with explicit weights, scoring, and assumptions.

Proposed factual anchors:

1. A weighted decision matrix multiplies each criterion's rating by its weight
   and adds the resulting scores for each option.
2. Rating scales need consistent direction: a higher score should mean a more
   desirable result, including for criteria such as cost.
3. ASQ cautions that ratings depend on the assumptions used to evaluate options.

Source: [American Society for Quality (ASQ): Decision matrix](https://asq.org/quality-resources/decision-matrix),
procedure, example, and considerations. Define ASQ on first use. Do not describe
the highest score as an objectively correct decision or claim weights eliminate bias.

Planned artifact: a blank, reusable matrix with an explicitly illustrative worked
example and verifiable arithmetic. Example inputs will be labeled assumptions,
not customer data, research findings, or recommended universal weights.

## 3. Pairwise comparison method for team decisions

Type: article. Series: guides. Query: `pairwise comparison method`.
Angle: explain head-to-head comparisons, a worked example, and method limitations.

Proposed factual anchors:

1. Pairwise comparison evaluates two alternatives at a time.
2. Comparing every distinct pair once requires n(n−1)/2 comparisons: five options
   give 10 pairs. This count is for a single complete set, not a ChoiceBoard
   question-count promise or a claim about multiple participants/factors.
3. Pairwise preferences can be inconsistent: preferring A to B and B to C does
   not ensure a preference for A over C.

Source: [1000minds: Pairwise comparison method](https://www.1000minds.com/decision-making/pairwise-comparison),
definition, complete-pair formula, and transitivity discussion. This is a vendor's
methodology explanation. Use it for these narrow methodological points, not as
independent evidence of product superiority. The five-option arithmetic is directly
checkable: 5 × 4 ÷ 2 = 10.
