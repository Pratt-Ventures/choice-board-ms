How it works

How to make a group decision in four steps

No training, no facilitation degree, no 40-tab spreadsheet. Frame the decision once — ChoiceBoard handles the structure, the math, and the diplomacy.

1

Frame the decision

Name your project, list the options on the table, and add the factors that matter — or keep it simple with an overall comparison.

2

Invite anyone

Share a private link or send email invites. Participants need zero accounts and zero onboarding — they just click and go.

3

Compare, one pair at a time

Everyone answers simple head-to-head questions: “Which option is better for cost?” Ten seconds each, on any device.

4

Get a defensible answer

A statistically ranked result with confidence, agreement, and what drove the outcome — ready to present, share, or export.

Why teams switch

Your decision matrix, minus the spreadsheet fights

A weighted decision matrix sounds rigorous — until five people invent scores from thin air, anchor on the loudest voice, and argue over cell C7. ChoiceBoard gets you the rigor without the theater.

No made-up numbers

Nobody has to decide whether “flexibility” is a 7 or an 8. Simple either-or judgments do the work, and the model computes the scores.

No anchoring, no groupthink

Everyone compares independently, so the quiet expert counts as much as the confident VP. Disagreement is surfaced, not steamrolled.

Weights earned, not guessed

Factor importance comes from the same head-to-head process — so “what matters most” reflects your team, not one person's gut.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is group decision-making software? +

It's software that gives a team a structured way to choose between options together. Instead of long meetings and biased spreadsheets, ChoiceBoard collects each person's input through simple head-to-head comparisons and combines them into a single ranked result — with confidence and agreement metrics so you know how solid the answer is.

How is ChoiceBoard different from a weighted decision matrix spreadsheet? +

A weighted decision matrix combines scores for options with weights for criteria. ChoiceBoard collects head-to-head comparisons instead of asking participants for numeric scores. The result includes a ranking and a measured level of agreement. ChoiceBoard doesn’t hide disagreement, it measures it.

What is pairwise comparison? +

A pairwise comparison is a judgment between two options at a time. In ChoiceBoard, participants answer head-to-head questions about options and factors. The comparisons contribute to a ranked result with a measured level of agreement.

Do participants need an account to give input? +

No. You invite participants with a private share link or email invitation. They open it on any device, answer one question at a time, and are done in minutes. You control exactly what each person can see — from compare-only up to the full results report — and every link can be revoked or expired.

What kinds of decisions is ChoiceBoard good for? +

Any decision with multiple options and multiple stakeholders: vendor and software selection, feature and roadmap prioritization, hiring panels, budget allocation, naming and creative reviews, and strategy trade-offs. Use Pick-one mode when you need a single winner, or Rank-all mode when you need a full priority order.
