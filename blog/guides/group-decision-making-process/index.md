# Group decision making process for teams

Your team has options, opinions, and another meeting on the calendar. A group decision making process gives that conversation a structure: define the call, decide who makes it, compare the options, and record the reasoning. Start with a clear question before you collect another vote.

## How do you make a decision as a group without endless meetings?

For a group decision, agree on the question, decision owner, and evaluation factors before gathering input. Use the meeting to examine unresolved trade-offs and make the call. The workflow below is our practical recommendation, not a claim about a guaranteed reduction in meeting time.

A group decision-making process is a sequence for gathering people’s input and reaching a choice. The group can contribute without every participant having the same decision authority. Make that distinction explicit at the start.

1. ### Frame the decision

  Write the decision as a question with a clear boundary. For example: “Which of these options should we choose for the next project?” Add the deadline, the constraints, and what would make an option unacceptable. This is a hypothetical prompt, not a customer example.

  Keep separate decisions separate. Choosing a supplier and agreeing on next year’s budget need different questions, even when the same people attend.
 
2. ### Assign the decision roles

  One available framework is Driver, Approver, Contributors, and Informed (DACI). In [Atlassian’s DACI play](https://www.atlassian.com/team-playbook/plays/daci), the Driver organizes the work, one Approver makes the decision, Contributors provide expertise, and Informed stakeholders receive the outcome.

  If you choose a different authority model, write that down instead. Tell participants whether they are advising, voting, or jointly approving. Do this before their preferred option is known.
 
3. ### Compare the same options against the same factors

  Prepare a shared description of each option. List the factors you want people to consider, then attach the evidence available for each one. Atlassian’s decision document similarly calls for relevant data, decision factors, and a table of options.

  Ask participants to record their judgments and reasons before the discussion. Leave room for “I don’t know.” A missing answer is a prompt to gather evidence, not an invitation to invent a score.
 
4. ### Review dissent and record the call

  Bring the unresolved differences into the conversation. Ask whether people disagree about the evidence, the importance of a factor, or the acceptable trade-off. Keep those questions separate as you work through them.

  Then have the designated owner make the call. Atlassian’s follow-up guidance recommends documenting the final decision so future teams can understand how and why it was reached. Record the remaining concerns alongside the outcome.

## What should a team decision record include?

A team decision record should let someone reconstruct the choice without attending the meeting. We recommend the following fields. Copy them into the document your team already uses.

Suggested decision record

| Field | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Decision and owner | The question, who makes the call, and the date. |
| Options and constraints | What was considered and which requirements had to be met. |
| Factors and evidence | What mattered, the supporting sources, and unresolved assumptions. |
| Outcome and dissent | The choice, its rationale, and the concerns that remain. |
| Next action | Who acts next and what new evidence would justify revisiting the choice. |

## How do you get buy-in on a decision people disagree with?

For a decision people disagree with, start by showing how their input was considered. Explain the trade-off the owner accepted and name any concern that remains open. Do not ask people to describe disagreement as consensus.

Our recommendation is to separate acknowledgment from agreement. A participant can confirm that the record represents their view while still preferring another option. Give them a way to correct the record before the team acts on it.

## Frequently asked questions

Who should make the final group decision?

Agree on decision authority before collecting input. In DACI, one Approver makes the final decision. Other processes may assign authority differently; tell participants which process you are using.

Does a group decision require everyone to agree?

That depends on the decision rule you choose. A process with a designated decision owner can collect group input without requiring unanimous approval. Record unresolved disagreement accurately.

What should you do when the team lacks evidence?

Record the gap, assign someone to investigate it, and state whether the decision can wait. We recommend keeping unknowns visible rather than filling them with unsupported scores.

What should you save after making a group decision?

Save the decision, owner, options, factors, supporting evidence, rationale, and remaining concerns. Add the next action and the conditions under which you would revisit the choice.
